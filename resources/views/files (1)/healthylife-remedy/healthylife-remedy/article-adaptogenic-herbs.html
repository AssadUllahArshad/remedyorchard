7 Adaptogenic Herbs That Actually Work for Stress Relief

JO
James Okafor, ND Naturopathic Doctor  •  June 18, 2026  •  7 min read

Adaptogens are a class of herbs believed to help the body resist physical, chemical, and biological stressors by supporting the adrenal system and regulating cortisol output. The term itself dates back to Soviet-era pharmacology research in the 1940s, but it's only in the past decade that rigorous, peer-reviewed clinical trials have started to catch up with the centuries of traditional use behind these plants.

Not every herb marketed as an "adaptogen" has solid evidence behind it. Here are seven that have meaningful human trial data supporting their use for stress and anxiety relief.

1. Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera)

The most extensively studied adaptogen by far. Multiple randomized controlled trials have found that ashwagandha root extract, typically dosed between 300–600mg daily, meaningfully reduces self-reported stress and anxiety scores compared to placebo, while also lowering morning cortisol levels in chronically stressed adults.

2. Rhodiola Rosea

Rhodiola has a long history of use among Siberian and Scandinavian populations to combat fatigue in harsh climates. Modern research focuses on its effect on mental fatigue and burnout symptoms, with several trials showing improved concentration and reduced exhaustion in people under chronic occupational stress.

3. Holy Basil (Tulsi)

Revered in Ayurvedic medicine for thousands of years, holy basil has shown promise in small human trials for reducing generalized anxiety and improving overall sense of wellbeing, likely through its effect on cortisol regulation and antioxidant activity.

"Adaptogens don't sedate you the way anti-anxiety medications can — they appear to help the body's stress-response system find its way back to baseline."

4. Panax Ginseng

Distinct from American ginseng, Panax (Korean/Asian) ginseng has research support for improving cognitive performance under stress and combating fatigue, particularly in populations dealing with chronic illness or recovery from medical treatment.

5. Schisandra Berry

Used in Traditional Chinese Medicine as a "five-flavor fruit," schisandra has demonstrated liver-protective and mild anti-fatigue properties in animal and limited human studies, with traditional use centered on improving resilience to physical and mental stress.

6. Maca Root

Native to the Peruvian Andes, maca is most commonly studied for its effects on mood and energy, with some evidence suggesting benefits for menopausal symptoms and mild depressive symptoms, although the stress-specific evidence is less robust than for ashwagandha or rhodiola.

7. Eleuthero (Siberian Ginseng)

Despite the name, eleuthero is botanically unrelated to true ginseng. It has a long history of use among Soviet athletes and astronauts for improving endurance and stress resilience, with modern research supporting modest improvements in immune function during periods of high stress.

A Word of Caution

Adaptogens can interact with thyroid medication, blood thinners, and immunosuppressants. Always talk to your doctor before starting any adaptogenic herb, particularly if you're pregnant, nursing, or managing a chronic condition.

The Bottom Line

Adaptogens are not a replacement for addressing the root causes of chronic stress — poor sleep, unsustainable workloads, or unresolved emotional difficulty. But as a complementary tool, several of these herbs now have genuine clinical evidence behind them, which is more than can be said for most of the wellness industry's stress-relief claims.
